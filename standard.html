HIC 6bCoreObjective Element

Hand hygiene is practised and monitored at every point of patient care.

Part of HIC 6 — Preventing Hospital-Associated Infections. The single most audited, most failed, and most preventable element in the chapter.

01

The standard

"The organisation adopts and implements standard precautions, with particular emphasis on hand hygiene practices, at all points of patient care."Representative wording shown for design. Official NABH text is inserted verbatim once reproduction rights are confirmed; otherwise this reads as an accredited paraphrase with citation.
02

In plain English

Everyone who touches a patient — or the things around a patient — cleans their hands at the right moments, every time. Not just doctors and nurses: housekeeping, physiotherapists, phlebotomists, porters.

"The right moments" is the WHO framework of five moments: before touching a patient, before a clean/aseptic task, after body-fluid exposure risk, after touching a patient, and after touching a patient's surroundings.

03

What the assessor looks for

The assessor's lens
  • Alcohol hand-rub actually present and full at the point of care — not just at the ward entrance.
  • Hand-hygiene compliance audit records — and evidence that low scores triggered action.
  • Staff can demonstrate the technique and name the five moments when asked directly.
  • Training records covering all cadres, including new joiners and support staff.
04

Common gap → the NC it becomes

The gap

Compliance is measured monthly and looks fine on paper — but dispensers sit empty at three bedsides, and no record shows the last two low scores were acted on.

The fix

A refill-and-check log at every point of care, plus a closed-loop CAPA trail: each dip in compliance links to an action, an owner, and a re-check.

05

How to comply

  1. Place alcohol hand-rub at every point of care and log refills daily.
  2. Run monthly WHO five-moments compliance audits, by ward and by cadre.
  3. Feed every below-target score into a CAPA with an owner and a due date.
  4. Refresh training on induction and annually — support staff included.
06

Your department's part

Nathan · Housekeeping

You keep the hand-rub flowing. Refill dispensers before they run dry, sign the point-of-care check log, and clean sinks and dispenser nozzles on schedule. When an assessor lifts a dispenser and it's empty, that's the finding — and it's yours to prevent.

Ryan · Human Resources

You own the evidence of competence. Every cadre — clinical and support — needs a hand-hygiene training record on induction and annually, with a signed competency assessment. A missing record for a single housekeeping hire is enough to open an NC.

Dr. Santhosh · Hospital Director

You own the closed loop. Compliance data should reach your quality review, and every below-target ward should show an action you can point to. Assessors don't fail hospitals for a low score — they fail them for a low score that nobody acted on.
